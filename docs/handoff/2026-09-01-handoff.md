## 1. What a shopper was actually getting

Five faults, all shipped in PR #7. Every one was invisible to any check that reads captured HTML,
because the markup is correct and the damage happens in the browser afterwards. They were found by
running the pages, not by reading them.

### srcset — 29 blank photographs per page, 185 pages

`app/lib/plan-b/same-origin-assets.ts`

A `srcset` separates candidates with commas, so the code split on commas to rewrite each URL onto
our storage, then rejoined with `", "`. **A `data:` URI contains a comma.** Her theme ships a tiny
inline SVG as a placeholder while the real photo loads:

```
hers:  data:image/svg+xml;utf8,<svg…>
ours:  data:image/svg+xml;utf8, <svg…>
                              ↑ inserted by us
```

In a srcset a space begins the size descriptor, so the candidate became nonsense — and a browser
prefers `srcset` over `src`, so our correctly re-hosted photo was **never considered**. Worse, the
rejoined string always differed from the original, so it was written back even on pages where no URL
needed rewriting at all.

Affected **bag-crush (49 pages)** and **thenicheshop (136 pages)**. The stored captures were
correct; the corruption happened at serve time, in the last step before the browser. That is why
every check that reads captured HTML passed these stores for weeks.

Fix: skip any srcset containing `data:`. Nothing in a data URI can be proxied anyway.

### `/variants/<id>?section_id=` — a 404 that killed the theme's JavaScript

`app/lib/plan-b/store-host.ts`, new `app/api/plan-b/section/route.ts`

bag-crush's theme asks for the in-store-pickup section on every product view. We answered **404 with
15 bytes of plain text**. The theme parsed it, called `.querySelector()` on nothing, threw — and
every line of its startup after that never ran.

Her own site answers with 207 bytes: a wrapper div and two empty `<template>` tags. She has no
pickup locations, so the section is empty on her site too; the theme only needs it to *exist*.

Routed on numeric ids only (`/^\/variants\/\d+$/`) so a real page at `/variants/anything` stays a
page, and allowed by NAME in `isAllowedStoreApi` — never by the `/api/plan-b/` prefix, since the
rest of that namespace must stay refused on sellers' domains.

### `<sale-price>` — a $200 price on a dress the cart charges $125 for

`app/lib/live-price.ts`

Shopify's newer themes mark a price up as:

```html
<price-list class="price-list price-list--product">
  <sale-price class="h4 text-on-sale">$200.00 USD</sale-price>
  <compare-at-price class="line-through">$498.00 USD</compare-at-price>
</price-list>
```

Our selectors matched `[class*='price']`. `class="h4 text-on-sale"` says nothing about price, so the
one element holding the money was never matched; `price-list` matched but its direct children are
elements, not text. `<compare-at-price>` was likewise missed by `[class*='compare-at']`.

feathers served a Karl Lagerfeld dress at its **crawl-day $200** with a stale $498 markdown, while
the item record and her live site both said **$125**. Fixed by adding the tags: `sale-price`,
`price-list`, `compare-at-price`.

Verified live: `$200.00 ×4, $498.00 ×5, $125 nowhere` → `$125.00 ×4, $498.00 ×1` (the remaining
$498 is ours, a correct compare-at matching her feed).

### `<data-island src>` — 72 images lost at cancellation

`app/lib/rehost-theme-assets.ts`

Asset collection asked for `script[src]` and `img[src]` — tag-scoped. thenicheshop's theme boots its
quick-buy island from `<data-island src="…island-quick-buy.bundle.js">`, a custom element, so
neither matched and the file was never copied. Under blackout that collection page dropped from
**162 loaded images to 90**.

Now collects `src` from **any** element; the existing extension test keeps it honest, so an
`<iframe>` pointing at a page is still ignored.

### Grid size — collections could not grow past crawl day

`app/lib/site-capture.ts`, `app/site/[slug]/[[...path]]/route.ts`

`injectLiveGrids` capped every grid at the number of cards the crawl happened to photograph. That is
right for a **rail** — one was once handed 251 products and its carousel could not render at all —
and wrong for a grid that **is** a collection, where the count is an accident of timing.

awoke's denim page was captured holding 36 cards; she has since filed 41. The cap now lifts only
where the caller says the grid is the collection (`uncapped[i]`, set when the grid's items came from
the collection filing rather than its own captured handles).

> **Three of these five are the same mistake**: selectors that assume classes and known tags,
> against themes that name things with custom elements. Worth a sweep for other places that assume
> the same.

## 2. The audit was calling correct stores broken

The fleet graded **six stores FAIL**. Not one was showing a price a shopper would not have been
charged, or a piece she could not buy. Four faults in the grader (`app/lib/store-health.ts`,
`app/lib/collection-parity.ts`, `app/lib/parity-regions.ts`, `scripts/parity-check.mts`):

**Prices, graded by the wrong check.** Two exist:

- `priceStale` — the rendered price against the record the cart will charge. **Exact.** Reported
  15/15 matching on every store re-run. Stays blocking.
- `pricesPresent` — every money-shaped string on her page diffed against ours. Cannot tell a wrong
  price from her page differing between two reads. **Was blocking; now degrading.**

On loved-again a hand check found **all fourteen** of her prices present on ours while the run said
14 of 15. On chill-boutique the "missing prices" — `$29, $167, $98, $9` — were the prices of four
products in a "most loved" app strip her homepage shows and ours does not: one difference, counted
twice. The re-tiering still leaves feathers' genuine mispricing blocking, which is the test of
whether it was right.

**Our own bag counted as a price.** We inject a cart with a running total; empty it reads `$0.00` —
a price on our page with no counterpart on hers, every page, every store. Now excluded via
`VOLATILE_SELECTOR`.

**"Collections showing pieces you didn't put in them"** — where the pages were showing **fewer**,
not more. Each rail now mirrors its seller's behaviour with sold pieces; where she drops them ours
drops them, while the filing keeps them because it records what *belongs* in the collection. feathers
was blocked over three rails whose gaps were 1, 3 and 3 — exactly their sold counts. The check now
accepts either legitimate answer (the whole filing, or just its live pieces) and still reports
anything else, which is what it exists for: a 94-piece rail once went out serving 401.

**"We couldn't compare the products"** on a page with no products on **either** side.
hachi-archive's homepage is a lookbook; headings 2/2, nav 1/1, prices 1/1, images 8 vs 8 — it matched
exactly. Nothing to compare is not the same as could not compare. The check also ran *before* the
product-page branch and `continue`d past it, so product pages — whose only product links are their
recommendations strip — reported as unreadable instead of matching.

**A price mismatch is now confirmed with a second look** before being reported: one extra page load,
only when something looks wrong. "Your store shows the wrong price" is the most alarming thing this
can tell a seller and the most expensive to be wrong about.

**Result on the same stored data: 6 FAIL → 1 real, and hachi-archive → PASS.**

## 3. The fleet run

`VERIFY_PORT=3000 nohup caffeinate -i scripts/fleet.sh > .verify/fleet.log 2>&1 &`

21 stores, six steps each, **5h55m**. Results in `.verify/FLEET-REPORT.md`, findings grouped in
`.verify/CENSUS.md`, per-store JSON and screenshots under `.verify/<slug>/`.

**~1.5 GB of theme assets recovered**, the largest being thenicheshop 629 MB, hachi 293 MB,
sourcedbyscottie 120 MB, vintage-archives-la 95 MB, feathers 87 MB.

> **A repair alone leaves stores worse off.** `importProductsAsItems` writes the seller's own image
> URLs back over our copies, so a repair *undoes* photo re-hosting. `fleet.sh` documents this and
> orders the steps accordingly. Never run `repair-store.mts` on its own without following it with a
> re-host. This morning's 20-store re-repair left the fleet partly un-hosted for about seven hours.

**Most of the six hours was the laptop sleeping, not the work.** Stores take ~5 minutes each;
bag-crush's 83 minutes and shop-vintage-charm's 88 were almost entirely stall time. `caffeinate -i`
does not hold on **battery power** — macOS drops the assertion and `PreventSystemSleep` reads 0.
Plug in, or steps will hang with dead sockets and never recover.

A watchdog for this is at `scratchpad/watchdog.sh` in the session directory: kills any fleet step
whose CPU time has not moved in 10 minutes, so the run advances instead of sitting there. Every step
is idempotent, so a killed one simply gets redone.

## 5. Capture version history

`app/lib/capture-versions-core.ts`, `capture-versions-db.ts`, `app/api/admin/capture-versions/route.ts`

`site_captures` holds one row per page and every write overwrote it. Only the seller's own edit had
any net under it; the two writes that run over **every page on every repair** — a re-crawl and asset
re-hosting — had none.

All three writers now snapshot first. **Gzipped**, and that is not an optimisation: the captures are
748 MB of a 1.1 GB database, so three plain copies would have tripled it. Measured 7:1 to 10.6:1 on
real pages — about **320 MB for three versions** instead of 2.2 GB.

Three deep, and **the most recent `crawl` always survives eviction**: the re-hosting pass touches
every page on every repair, so without that exception three mechanical rewrites would evict the
actual capture of her site.

The seller's undo reads only her own edits — she steps back through her changes, not our plumbing —
and is a proper stack now. The operator's view reads everything. `previous_html` is retired rather
than left beside it as a second history; it held 0 rows.

Ordering is tie-broken on the BIGSERIAL id: Postgres returned whole-second timestamps in the first
live run, so three rewrites in the same second were indistinguishable by time and the pruner could
have evicted the newest version.

**Not built: the admin recovery UI.** The API exists. Design decided but unwritten — lead with a
triage list of pages whose served size has *shrunk* against their newest version (the signature of a
bad import: 850 kB becomes 12 kB), preview in `<iframe sandbox srcdoc>` with **no** `allow-scripts`
and **no** `allow-same-origin`, restore behind an explicit confirm. Needs one endpoint returning
per-store path / live size / newest version size.

## 9. Things worth knowing before you touch this

**Never edit serve-path files while a fleet, walk or parity run is going.** A Next recompile changes
the code underneath the run. This invalidated three runs in one day.

**The harness lies more often than the stores do.** Of thirteen failures one walk reported, eleven
were the harness. Of three the next one reported, **all three** were. Three of its bugs are fixed in
PR #7 — a hit-test clamped to the viewport edge (a button 45 px below the fold read as unreachable),
sign-out clicked without opening the account panel (its button sits 82 px off-screen while closed),
and an empty recommendations block called a failure without checking whether her page has one.

**Measure the page, not the markup.** Every fault in §1 passed a markup check. The one that found
the srcset bug was comparing *one image's attributes* against the same image on her site.

**`0/0` is a match, not a failure.** It cost hachi-archive a WARN for weeks.

**A green blackout tick does not mean images work** — you cannot lose at cancellation what never
loaded in the first place.
